  f_fuzziness   = FUZZINESS_WEIGHT[claim.specificity]   {'snapshot': 1.0, 'version': 0.6, 'family': 0.3}
  f_specificity = specificity_factor(version_named, has_numbers,
                                     has_conditions, has_repro_steps)
                  version_named -> +0.2, numbers -> +0.2,
                  conditions -> +0.2, repro -> +0.4, then 0.3 + 0.7*score

  version_named comes from document.names_version (pipeline.py:223),
  computed by collect/triage/specificity.py:names_version(text, aliases)
  over the WHOLE document. claim.specificity is about the surface THIS
  claim used. Different granularity, and contract/harvest.yaml already
  warns about exactly this pair: 'Different granularity (document vs
  claim) ... They must never be merged AND NEVER COMPARED'.

==============================================================================
DOES A FAMILY CLAIM OUTWEIGH A VERSION-NAMED ONE?
==============================================================================
  reference: a VERSION claim, version_named, no artifacts   0.2244

  every FAMILY combination that beats it (reddit, tier A, fresh, central):
    0.2550  family + version_named,numbers,conditions,repro

  the case the finding is about - family claim with real artifacts:
    0.2193  family, numbers+conditions+repro, version_named FALSE
    0.2550  family, numbers+conditions+repro, version_named TRUE
    0.1836  family, repro only, version_named TRUE
    0.2244  version, version_named only (the reference)
    0.3740  snapshot, version_named only

  So the overtaking is REAL and it needs version_named=True on a
  family claim - which is not a contradiction in the code, because
  version_named is a fact about the DOCUMENT. Any family claim in a
  document that names a version anywhere gets the +0.2.

==============================================================================
IS version_named DERIVABLE FROM claim.specificity?
==============================================================================
  Mechanically yes: specificity in ('snapshot','version') <=> a version
  was named by the surface this claim used. One expression.

  But substituting it is NOT a one-line fix, for two reasons:

  1. IT DOUBLE-COUNTS. f_specificity would give +0.2 for the same fact
     f_fuzziness already prices at 0.6/1.0. A version claim would go
     from 0.2244 to 0.2244 (unchanged) but a family
     claim from 0.1122 to 0.0765 - a 22% cut applied twice
     for one fact. The 0.2 and the 0.3 were chosen independently and
     neither was calibrated.

  2. IT CHANGES WHAT f_specificity MEASURES. Its docstring is 'real
     experience carries numbers and error strings; slop carries
     adjectives' - a DOCUMENT-QUALITY score. version_named is in it as a
     cheap proxy for 'this person was being specific', not as an
     attribution signal. Removing it narrows the factor to three
     artifact signals, which may be right, and is a decision about what
     the factor is for.

  So: a one-line source change is AVAILABLE and the question of whether
  to make it is a design question. The defect is not that the wrong
  variable is passed - it is that one fact is priced twice at two
  granularities, and nobody chose that.
