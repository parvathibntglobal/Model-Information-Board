# AI terms: 289 → 772 (2026-11-27)

**What changed.** The Industry AI lane's `ai_terms` grew from 289 to 772. All 483 new
terms are at the end of the list in `contract/industry_ai.yaml`, after a comment that
names the sources. Every term, its group, its hits and every source that named it are in
`ai-terms-2026-11-27.tsv` beside this file.

**Why.** A post is kept only if it names an AI term. A short list drops posts about AI
that name a library, a benchmark or a technique instead of "AI" or a model. anooj asked
for at least 1,000 terms from reputable sources, each checked for relevance.

**The result is 772, not 1,000.** The rest of this note says why. In short: the terms
needed to reach 1,000 are words with an ordinary meaning ("transformer", "inference",
"Cursor", "Whisper"). The matcher reads every term as a whole word in any case, so it
cannot tell those apart from everyday English. See *What would reach 1,000*.

## Method

1. **Collected** 3,272 distinct candidates (case folded) from 62 sources. 169 were
   already in the list, which left 3,103 new ones.
   - Standards and government: NIST AI 100-3 and AI 100-2e2025, the JRC human-centric AI
     glossary (EUR 31113), the EU AI Act Art. 3, the EU-U.S. Terminology and Taxonomy
     for AI, the ICO, US DOT ITS JPO.
   - Universities: Stanford HAI (glossary and the 2025 AI Index), MIT Sloan, CMU
     Libraries, Harvard (Belfer Center, Library), the Open University.
   - Vendors: Google's ML glossary, Hugging Face, AWS, Databricks, NVIDIA, IBM,
     Anthropic.
   - Wikipedia: its AI glossaries and lists (glossary, outline, LLMs, chatbots,
     datasets, deep-learning software, and others).
   - Curated lists for products: Awesome-LLM, awesome-generative-ai, and the official
     sites and repositories of the tools.
   - Contribution to the 483 kept: Google's ML glossary named 97, the two GitHub lists 71
     and 62, Wikipedia's outline of AI 38, and Stanford HAI, the JRC and NIST 18–19
     each. 120 of the 483 were named by two or more sources.
2. **Removed the covered ones.** 380 candidates contain a term already in the list as
   whole words (for example "prompt injection attack" contains "prompt injection").
   The matcher already finds every post they would find, so they add nothing.
3. **Read the other 2,723 by hand**, against this bar: **the string's meaning is AI, and
   ordinary English, a person's name or another trade does not use the same string.**
   The bar is set by how the matcher reads a term: whole words, any case, a space,
   hyphen or underscore matching any of the three, and ".ai" removed from the text
   first. Not added:
   - **generic words:** data, model, agent, inference, GPU, transformer, guardrails,
     hallucination, token;
   - **product names that are words or names:** Bolt, Cursor, Whisper, Harvey, Devin,
     Sora, BERT;
   - **names ending in ".ai":** they can never match, because ".ai" is removed first;
   - **anything that needs context** to read as AI.

   This accepted 492, grouped as ML concepts, products and tools, and benchmarks and
   datasets.
4. **Measured the effect on the stored posts and read a sample** (shared DB, read-only;
   post text stayed in memory or in a private file outside the repo).
   - With the 492, 86 of the 5,106 posts dropped as "no AI term" (with text, and with
     no AI term under the 289 on their current text) would have gained one.
   - We read 40 of the 86: 32 used the term in its AI sense, 7 did not, 1 was unclear.
   - The 7 misses were student "evals", teaching "active learning", test fixtures as
     "synthetic data", an app's "cross-validation", and "make a video".
5. **Dropped 8** after that reading: Make-A-Video, active learning, evals, synthetic
   data, cross-validation, pre-training, Pretraining, Agent Skills. The last three
   have the same exposure (sport and teaching; a sales or insurance agent's skills).
   That left 484.
6. **Removed "data labeling"** because it is also a term of the active
   `data_analytics` domain, so one phrase would pass the AI test and the domain test
   at once and file general AI talk under that domain (#645;
   `test_no_active_domain_term_is_an_ai_term_by_itself` caught it).
   **483 kept** (ml_concepts 200, products_and_tools 244, benchmarks_and_datasets 39).

## Effect, with the final 772 (recounted 2026-11-27, shared DB, read-only)

Population: the `iai_item` rows whose text is not cleared, as filed by the iai-7
labels. The figures compare main's 289 terms with this branch's 772 on the same text:
title, body and media text, after `run.match_strip`.

| | count |
| --- | --- |
| posts dropped as "no AI term", with text | 5,159 |
| …with no AI term under main's 289 on their current text | 5,106 |
| …that name one of the 772 | **69 (1.4%)** |
| kept posts, with text | 16,819 |
| …that name at least one more AI term than before | 1,137 |

- **By platform**, the 69 are: Reddit 21, X 18, GitHub 17, Hacker News 5, Bluesky 4,
  Instagram 3, dev.to 1.
- **The terms doing most of it:** Embeddings 7, Hermes Agent 6, multimodal 4,
  OpenCode 4, Kaggle 4, Higgsfield 4.
- **For a kept post, more terms change nothing about whether it is kept.** It changes
  the terms recorded for it, which the post card and Info view show.
- **Of the 483, 235 name no stored post today**, either kept or "no AI term". They
  cover what is named in the field, not only what our sources have said so far.

The 69 are a different population from the 40 we read (86 posts, before the 8
drops), so 32 of 40 is not a precision figure for the final list. It is the reading
that led to the drops.

## Speed

Asking all 773 terms (before "data labeling" came out) for every post took 2.7
times as long as the 289 (24.3 s against
9.1 s for the same 2,000 stored posts). So `ai_terms_found` now asks only the terms
whose first word appears in the post as a word. A term cannot match where its first
word is absent, so the answer is the same.

- **Checked on all 34,861 stored posts with text:** 0 different answers. Asking every
  term took 522.6 s; the pre-filter took 40.6 s.
- **Pinned by `tests/test_industry_ai_ai_terms.py`** on strings built to break it:
  C++, `huggingface_hub`, DALL·E, a curly apostrophe, ".ai" domains, parentheses,
  non-Latin text, and every term upper-cased, lower-cased and hyphenated.
- **`term_pattern`'s cache is now unbounded.** It was 4,096. The AI terms, every
  domain's terms and the registry's names together could outgrow that, and an evicted
  pattern is recompiled on every post.

## What would reach 1,000

The candidates left out in step 3 include many that are clearly AI in an AI post and
ordinary words elsewhere (not counted separately: they were set aside, not tagged).
Examples: transformer, inference, GPU, guardrails, hallucination, Cursor, Whisper,
BERT, Sora, Devin.

Adding them as plain terms would keep posts about electrical transformers, legal
inference and a mouse cursor. They need a **context-gated tier**: a term that counts
only when an unambiguous AI term is within N words, or in the same post. That is code
and a contract shape we don't have. `ambiguous_terms` gates a domain's terms, not AI
terms, and it does not support phrases (Parvathi, #679). It is proposed separately, not
built here.
