# Pinned Testimony

*A visual philosophy. 19 Nov 2026. One direction among several: chosen for
topics built from individual reports about one subject, where the reader
should see each piece of testimony as its own object, and never for every
topic.*

**Every claim is a thing you could pick up.** Pinned Testimony imagines the
wall of an investigator who trusts nothing until it is written on a card and
fixed in place. Each report becomes a physical object: a card with weight,
an edge, a slight imperfection in how it was pinned. The arrangement is the
argument. What sits together belongs together, and what stands apart was
kept apart on purpose. The wall must look as though it was assembled over
weeks by someone painstaking, the product of deep expertise in both evidence
and craft.

**Ground and material.** The ground is dark, soft and absorbent, like felt
or cork seen in low light. It recedes completely so that the cards glow
against it. The cards are a warm off-white stock with the faint grain of real
paper. Pins are small, round and coloured, and they do double duty: each pin
is one person, so that a cluster of pins is a crowd you can count. Stamps,
pressed slightly off square in a dense ink, mark what each card says. Every
material choice is calibrated with meticulous restraint: nothing glossy,
nothing digital, nothing that could not exist on a real wall.

**Colour as verdict ink.** Two stamp inks and a muted grey: one ink for
testimony in favour, one for testimony against, and grey for testimony that
takes no side. The inks appear only in stamps and pins, never in flooding
fills, and always beside a word that says the same thing. The dark ground
carries no colour of its own beyond a quiet temperature, so the inks read as
the only voices in the room.

**Rhythm and placement.** The cards are aligned to an invisible grid and then
released from it by a degree or two, as a careful hand would pin them, never
scattered. Spacing is even. Rotation is slight and deliberate, and no card
overlaps another's words. Typed text sits on the cards in a monospaced face,
as though struck on a machine. A refined serif names the subject once, and a
handwritten note appears at most once per wall, where a person would naturally
add one. Master-level execution shows in the restraint: the fewest objects
that make the case.

**Hierarchy and honesty.** Read the wall from the subject to the crowd to the
individual voices. Each card carries its source and its date, stamped, so its
provenance cannot be separated from its content. The wall states plainly
what it does not hold, such as the period with no testimony, because an empty
stretch of wall is information too. The finished piece should feel like an
exhibit that could stand in a hearing: each element placed with countless
hours of care, and nothing on it that its owner could not defend.
