# Field Report

*A visual philosophy. 19 Nov 2026. One direction among several: it is chosen
for topics built on what people observed first-hand, not used for everything.*

**Observation made visible.** Field Report treats every image as a page torn
from a working notebook: the record of someone who went out, looked
carefully, and wrote down only what they saw. Its authority comes from
patience, not from polish. Each mark stands for something that happened
once: a tally stroke, a dot, a ruled line. The viewer should feel they could
recount it by hand and arrive at the same number. The work must look
meticulously kept, the product of someone who has filled a hundred such
notebooks and knows exactly where every mark belongs.

**Space and material.** The ground is paper with warmth in it, never pure
white, and never the dark glow of a screen. Fine ruling runs across the
page like a surveyor's grid, quiet enough to recede, and present enough to
say that this page was measured before anything was written on it. A single
margin rule divides the record from the annotation. Negative space is not
emptiness; it is the unmarked part of the survey, left open on purpose. Each
composition must look painstakingly laid out, with the calm of a page that
was planned before the pen touched it.

**Colour as instrument.** Two inks and one graphite. Blue ink for what was
recorded in favour, red pencil for what was recorded against, and soft
graphite for everything that frames them. Colour never decorates. It sorts.
Where two inks meet, a direct label always says which is which, so the
record survives a photocopy and a colour-blind reader alike. The palette is
calibrated with the restraint of a cartographer, every hue earned and none
added for mood.

**Scale and rhythm.** Quantity is shown as accumulation: one mark per
person, gathered in fives, so that a large number reads as a dense field and
a small one as a sparse patch. Rhythm comes from repetition held perfectly
steady: the same stroke, the same spacing, row after row. A hand-drawn
tremor is allowed in the marks and nowhere else. Type is never the spectacle
here. A serif headline with the gravity of a printed journal, small
monospaced figures like instrument readings, and an italic hand for marginal
notes. Every alignment is the result of countless refinements; master-level
execution shows in the spacing, not in ornament.

**Composition and hierarchy.** Read top to bottom like a specimen sheet. The
subject is named once, plainly. The evidence occupies the body of the page
as marks. The annotation sits in the margin, small and exact: what was
counted, over what, and when. Reference markers (a plate number, a page
count, a catalogue line) anchor the sheet to a larger archive. Nothing
overlaps, nothing crowds the edge, and nothing is said in words that the
marks already say. The finished page should feel like an artifact from a
discipline that takes human testimony as seriously as a naturalist takes a
specimen: studied, counted, labelled, kept.
