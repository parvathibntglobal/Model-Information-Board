# Signal Block

*A visual philosophy. 19 Nov 2026. One direction among several: chosen for
topics whose point is a number, such as a count across many subjects, a price,
or a change, and never for every topic.*

**The number is the image.** Signal Block treats a figure the way a poster
treats a face: it fills the frame, and everything else is placed in relation to
it. One numeral dominates each page, set so large that it becomes
architecture, its counters and stems acting as the walls of the composition.
Around it, smaller figures stand in strict ranks, and the eye moves from the
monument to its evidence in one motion. The page must look meticulously
engineered, the product of a designer who has set ten thousand numerals and
knows exactly how tight a condensed seven can sit beside a five.

**Colour as territory.** Colour arrives in flat, uncompromising fields: a
single saturated block that claims a whole region of the page, against a calm
light ground. Inside the data, colour is strictly functional: one hue for what
went wrong, one for what held, each always named in words beside the mark.
There are no gradients, no glows, no textures pretending to be depth. The
fields are cut with the precision of screen-printed stock, edges crisp,
proportions calculated, every block the result of painstaking adjustment.

**Scale and rhythm.** The rhythm is a drumbeat of extremes. One enormous
figure, then a row of large ones, then a quiet line of small type that says
exactly what was counted. Bars are thick and blunt, measured against one
axis that never changes between rows, so length can be compared at a glance.
The space between rows is generous and identical, a grid held with
master-level discipline, never loosened to fit one more thing in.

**Type.** A condensed grotesque carries every number and every headline,
upper case, tightly fitted, with the confidence of transit signage. A clean
humanist sans carries the few sentences. A small monospace carries the
provenance: the dates, the population, the path. No more than three voices,
each with one job, and none of them decorative.

**Hierarchy and honesty.** The loudest element is always a count, never an
adjective. Every large number has its denominator within reach. A smaller
note always says what the number is not: not a rating, and not a verdict on
anything that was not reported. Silence is shown as silence, an empty slot
named in words, never filled with a reassuring shape. The finished page should
look inevitable, as though the data had chosen its own layout and a master
craftsman had merely refused to get in its way.
