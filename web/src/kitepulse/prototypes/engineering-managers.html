Build your weekly routine

AI native engineering leadership is mostly habit: a look each morning, a planning check each week, a project review each month. Pick your planning day and what applies; the routine updates and copies as plain text.

Routine builder

Weekly AI team routine

Every morning
  1. Open The Office and look for stuck or plan-limit markers on your team's desks.
  2. On Team Overview, check who is coding now, active time against a typical day, and who needs attention.
Every Monday
  1. Record next week's leave and on-call rotas on Leave & On-Call.
  2. On the Plan headroom tab, check who is near or at a Claude plan limit before handing out heavy AI work.
  3. On Collaboration, look for projects where several people now work, and connect them.
  4. Ask an admin to grant Work Problems or Blocker Board on Roles & Permissions if you need them.
  5. If it is not set up yet, ask an admin to schedule the Company report weekly by email (email must be set up on the server).
Once a month
  1. On All Projects, review which projects went dormant and confirm with their owners.
  2. Read the Company report for the month against the month before.

What Kite Pulse shows you, and what it does not

Clear limits make rollout easier. See developer analytics without keystroke tracking for what developers accept.

What you see, for your reporting line

  • Who is coding, active time and current projects
  • Stuck work and plan-limit markers on The Office
  • Claude plan headroom, worst first
  • Token use per person, tool, model and project; estimated cost per tool, not billed spend
  • Project health, collaboration, leave and rotas

What you do not see

  • Detail for people outside your line
  • Private prompt statistics, or cost per developer (admin-only)
  • Plan limits for tools other than Claude Code
  • Agents in parallel for your reports: managers see it for themselves only
  • Keystrokes, screenshots or a project's source files; session records can include code excerpts, secrets masked
  • DORA metrics, which git and ticket based tools cover better

Questions from engineering managers

How can an engineering manager see where the team is stuck with AI coding tools?

Open The Office, where each desk in your reporting line carries a marker when AI work is stuck or a Claude plan limit is near. For repeated failures across the team, Work Problems groups failed commands, error messages and retries by severity with suggested actions; it is admin by default, and admins can grant it to managers.

Which people can a manager see in Kite Pulse?

A manager sees themselves and their whole reporting line, including indirect reports, and the server enforces this, not just the screen. On The Office, everyone else shows as presence only, with detail only for people in your scope.

Can a manager see Work Problems, Blocker Board and Organisation Overview?

Not by default. All three are admin pages by default, and an admin can grant each one to managers on Roles & Permissions. Blocker Board shows recurring causes and suggested actions, never a single person.

What will a manager not see about their reports?

Their private prompt statistics, which only the developer sees, and cost per developer, which is admin-only. Kite Pulse reads the AI tools' session records; it does not log keystrokes, take screenshots, track websites or open a project's source files.
