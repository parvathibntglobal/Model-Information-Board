AI Budgets: set an AI budget per team or project, with alerts

A monthly figure for each team or project, and a warning while there is still time to talk about it.

AI Budgets lets an admin set a monthly AI budget per team or per project, counted in tokens or in estimated US dollars, and raises an alert at 80% and again at 100%. A dollar budget adds up only the tools that have a price and lists unpriced tokens beside it, so nothing is counted as $0.

Seat prices and shared tool accounts sit next to it. For the method behind the figures, read how to track AI coding costs.

How much of each team's monthly AI budget is used?

AI Budgets is the LLM budget tracker for your coding tools: one list of every budget, what was used this month, a bar and a status. Admins set them under Settings.

  • One monthly budget per team or per project. Budgets belong to a team or project, never to one person.
  • Team budgets follow the membership on Teams, which keeps start and end dates, so a move between teams does not rewrite history.
  • Tokens or estimated US dollars, chosen per budget.
  • Alerts at 80% and at 100% of the month's budget.

Should an AI token budget count tokens or dollars?

A token budget counts use from every tool you collect. A dollar budget reads closer to how finance thinks, but it can only add up what has a price, and that price is an estimate from published list prices, labelled "not billed spend".

That matters most for a monthly AI budget per project that mixes tools:

ToolIn a dollar budget
Claude Code, Gemini CLIEstimated from list prices for Claude and Gemini models
Aider, Cline, Roo Code, Kilo Code, OpenCodeThe cost the tool reports itself
Codex, Cursor, GitHub Copilot Chat in VS CodeUsually no price; tokens listed separately as unpriced

A price change is added as a new dated entry, so earlier sessions keep the price they ran at. Outside AI Budgets, Team AI Usage shows estimated cost per tool and never adds it up across tools; cost per developer is shown to admins only.

When do token budget alerts fire, and who sees them?

  • At 80% of a budget, then at 100%, for token and dollar budgets alike.
  • Budgets are checked every hour, and alerts appear with Kite Pulse's other alerts: the in-app bell and email.
  • Email needs a mail server set up on your Kite Pulse server.
  • An alert is a notice. Kite Pulse reads the history the AI tools keep; it does not switch a tool off.

    What do the seats cost, and which accounts are shared?

    Token estimates miss the flat part of the bill, since many developers are on subscriptions. Two admin pages cover it.

    • Seat prices. Admins enter what each tool's seats cost on Hourly Rates & Seat Prices. Each change is a new dated entry, and seat prices then appear on the Team AI Usage Overview for admins.
    • Tool Accounts. How many separate signed-in accounts each tool runs under, with accounts used by more than one person marked "Shared". Shows plan tier, machines, last seen and email domain, for Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, OpenCode and GitHub Copilot. Admin by default.
    • Accounts arrive as a keyed hash, the email domain and the plan name, never the address. For GitHub Copilot, only a hashed account number.

    Questions about AI budgets for teams

    What is an AI budget per team?

    It is a monthly limit on how much AI coding a team uses, counted in tokens or in estimated US dollars. In Kite Pulse an admin sets it on AI Budgets, and alerts are raised when the team reaches 80% and 100% of it. Budgets belong to a team or a project, not to a single person.

    Can I set a monthly AI budget per project?

    Yes. A budget is set for a team or for a project and works the same way for both: a monthly amount, used month to date, with alerts at 80% and 100%.

    Why does a dollar budget list unpriced tokens separately?

    A dollar budget can only add up tools that have a price. Claude and Gemini models are priced from list prices, and Aider, Cline, Roo Code, Kilo Code and OpenCode report their own cost. Codex, Cursor and Copilot usually have no price, so their tokens are reported beside the budget instead of being counted as $0.

    Does a budget stop developers at 100%?

    No. An AI budget raises alerts; it does not switch off an AI tool. Kite Pulse reads the session history the tools keep, so the decision about what happens next stays with people.

    Is the dollar figure what we are billed?

    No. It is an estimate from published list prices, labelled not billed spend. Many developers are on flat subscriptions, which is why seat prices are entered and shown separately.

    Does AI Budgets read our OpenRouter budget?

    No. AI Budgets counts tokens from the coding tools' own session history. Kite Pulse uses an OpenRouter key only if an admin turns on AI labelling, which is off by default and has its own monthly spending cap with an alert at 80%.
