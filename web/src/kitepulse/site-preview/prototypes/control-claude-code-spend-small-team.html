A small team has two AI limits, and they fail differently

Most founders think of AI coding cost as one bill. With Claude Code there are two things to watch, and they hurt in different ways.

Money

The estimated cost of tokens

How many tokens each tool used, priced at the vendor's published list price. It tells you where usage is heading and which project is using it. It is not your invoice.

Time

Claude plan windows

Each developer's plan has a 5-hour window, a weekly window and a weekly window per model. When one fills up, that person stops getting work out of Claude Code until it resets.

Many teams pay for Claude Code through flat per-seat plans, so the money side grows slowly and predictably while the time side is where things go wrong. A developer who hits a weekly limit on Wednesday afternoon loses the rest of the week's AI help, and on a small team that is a large share of your capacity. Claude Code team usage limits are a planning problem before they are a cost problem.

Watch plan headroom and token pace every week

Plan headroom on Team AI Usage shows who is close to a Claude plan limit, worst first, across the 5-hour window, the weekly window and each model's weekly window. "Near" means 80% or more, and "at limit" means 100%. Old readings are flagged as stale rather than hidden, so a quiet machine does not look like spare room.

The figures come from the usage Claude Code saves on each developer's computer; no login or password is read. By default they are as fresh as the last time Claude Code saved them. An admin can switch on a background refresh that keeps them current without using any tokens.

Plan headroom covers Claude Code only. Other tools show "Not reported by this tool". If you want to understand how the windows themselves behave, read our guide to Claude Code usage limits.

For the money side, Team AI Usage shows token use per developer, tool, model, project and day, so a heavy day stands out on the daily view.

A short weekly routine

  1. Open Plan headroom. Anyone near a weekly limit early in the week gets the smaller tasks, and the large one moves to after their reset.

  2. Look at tokens by day for heavy days, then open that day's sessions to see what drove it.

  3. Look at the budget alerts below, if any fired.

Set AI coding budget alerts per team or project

A budget turns a worry into an alert. In AI Budgets, an admin sets a monthly budget per team or per project, in tokens or in estimated US dollars. Alerts fire at 80% and at 100%. Budgets are set for a team or a project, not for a person.

Three things to know before you pick dollars over tokens:

  • Cost is an estimate. Tokens are multiplied by the vendor's published list price, and the figure is labelled "not billed spend". Claude and Gemini models are priced. Aider, Cline, Roo Code, Kilo Code and OpenCode report their own cost, and Kite Pulse uses it.
  • Some tools have no price. Codex, Cursor and GitHub Copilot Chat in VS Code usually show a dash, never $0. A dollar budget adds up the priced tools and reports unpriced tokens separately.
  • Cost stays per tool. Team AI Usage shows estimated cost per tool and never adds it up across tools. Cost per developer is shown to admins only.

For a team that uses Claude Code almost exclusively, a dollar budget is a fair early warning. For a team that mixes tools, a token budget may say more. Our AI cost tracking guide walks through estimating spend tool by tool.

Choosing a Claude plan for each engineer on the team

Picking a Claude plan for engineering team members is a guess until you have a few weeks of history. The Plan usage report gives that history per developer and per Claude account: weekly peaks, weeks at 80% or more, weeks and 5-hour windows at the limit, and per-model peaks. It downloads as Excel for admins, and history starts when recording began.

What the report showsWhat it suggests
Weeks at the limit, oftenThis person's work needs more plan room, or their heaviest tasks need spreading across the week.
5-hour windows at the limitBursty days. Breaking big tasks up may help more than a bigger plan.
Weekly peaks rarely near 80%The current plan has room to spare.

Two other views help with seats. Tool Accounts shows how many separate accounts each tool runs under, the plan tier, and accounts used by more than one person, marked "Shared". Hourly Rates & Seat Prices lets an admin enter what each tool's seats cost, so seat prices sit beside usage on the Team AI Usage Overview.

Kite Pulse does not recommend a plan or know Anthropic's current prices. You make the call; the report gives you the evidence.

Spot the parts of the product only one person works on

On a small team, one person's empty plan window can stall a whole area of the product. All Projects lists every project with its contributors, sessions and last activity, and Collaboration shows the projects several people work on. A project that never appears in Collaboration, with one name in its contributors, is a part of the product only one person touches.

If that person runs out of plan room, nobody else is warmed up on the code, so plan their heavy weeks first. Leave & On-Call records holidays, so a quiet week of leave is not misread.

What very small teams will not see

Some team measures are pooled across people on purpose, and they wait for enough people before showing anything. Team Review holds back small teams, and AI Collaboration needs a minimum team size before a team appears. Both also need AI labelling, which is off until an admin sets it up. Trends draws a gap for a week with too little data, and personal insights say "not enough data" instead of guessing.

For a very small team, that means the plan, token and budget views above do most of the work, and the pooled team pages fill in as you grow. If one founder uses one tool and never comes near a limit, a weekly look at your own Workspace figures may be all you need for now.
