Outputs and process: two sources of engineering intelligence

Most software engineering intelligence reads outputs: commits, pull requests, tickets and survey answers. They tell you what shipped and how long it took. The process behind them, the approaches tried and dropped, the errors and retries, now happens inside AI coding sessions. Here is one feature seen both ways.

    How Kite Pulse works as an engineering intelligence platform

    A small background program on each developer's computer picks up the session history the AI coding assistants already keep, strips out secrets, and sends it to your team's own Kite Pulse server. It covers Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Cursor, GitHub Copilot, Cline, Roo Code, Kilo Code, OpenCode and Aider. For scaling AI adoption in engineering, and scaling agentic coding across your organisation, that means one view of every tool rather than one console per vendor, with AI cost per person, project, tool and period.

    Kite Pulse

    Its guiding rule is "historian, not critic": it records and describes what happened, and never passes judgement on a person. When there is too little data, an insight says "not enough data" rather than guessing.

    It does not open or upload a project's source files. Data goes only to the server your team configured; there is no vendor cloud in between. A manager sees only their own reporting line, enforced by the server.

    Engineering intelligence platform: questions

    What is an engineering intelligence platform?

    An engineering intelligence platform turns data from software engineering work into a picture leaders can act on: what the team is working on, where work gets stuck, how AI tools are used and what they cost. It is also called software engineering intelligence, or a developer productivity insight platform. The term is also used for hardware and product engineering data platforms, which are a different market.

    How do I know what my engineering team is working on right now?

    Use a live view built from the work itself rather than from status updates: who is active, on which project, and since when. Kite Pulse's Org Overview shows who is online, coding, idle or offline, sessions today, active time, a timeline of each person's day and their current project, built from the AI coding sessions the team is already running.

    How do I see what my team is working on without status meetings?

    Replace the round of updates with a shared view everyone can read before the meeting: current project per person, and which projects several people touched. Kite Pulse's Collaboration page shows each shared project, the people contributing and how many sessions each spent there, so stand-ups can be about blockers instead of status.

    How can I see team activity in real time?

    Real-time activity needs a source that updates as people work. Commits and tickets update when work is finished; AI coding sessions update while it happens. Kite Pulse's Org Overview is a live control room of who is coding, idle or offline and on which project, within a manager's own reporting line.

    How can managers spot struggling developers early without surveys?

    Look at the work, not at output counts: very long sessions with little progress, the same command failing again and again, and approaches started and dropped. Treat each as a reason for a supportive check-in, not a judgement, and check leave and on-call first. When data is thin, say so rather than guessing.

    How do I spot developers who are stuck in long AI sessions?

    A stuck session is long, and shows repeated failures or the assistant retrying the same fix. Session history records both. Kite Pulse's Error & Command Stats shows failing commands, repeated failure loops and the most common error types across the team, which usually points at a shared cause such as a broken test setup.

    How do I identify developers who need support or coaching with AI tools?

    Look for patterns that a tip or a fix would change: the assistant retrying one fix many times, recurring setup failures, or many very short sessions in a day. Offer help to the team first, because the cause is often shared. Never turn these signals into a ranking of people.

    How do I account for leave and on-call when measuring AI usage?

    Record them next to the usage data. Without that, a week of leave looks like disengagement and a support rota looks like a focus problem. Kite Pulse's Leave & On-Call page records holidays and duty rotas for exactly this reason.

    Which teams are getting the most value from AI coding tools?

    Usage alone cannot tell you: the team using AI most may simply have the most AI-shaped work. Compare delivery and quality metrics for each team against its own baseline from before rollout, and read usage next to them.

    How can I identify teams that need more AI training?

    Look for teams where AI sessions often loop: repeated failing commands, the same fix retried, approaches dropped. Those usually point to missing project context, setup problems or tasks that are too large, which training on how to brief an assistant can fix. Low adoption on its own may instead mean the tools do not fit that team's work.

    Does an engineering intelligence platform need access to our source code?

    It depends on the platform. Git-based platforms read repository and pull request data. Kite Pulse does not open or upload source files: it reads the session history AI coding tools already keep, plus folder names and repository addresses to tell projects apart, and removes secrets on the developer's computer before upload.
