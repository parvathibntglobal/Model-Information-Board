01

Start with what your team actually uses

AI coding costs come from two places: seats (a flat monthly price per developer) and usage (pay-per-token billing, or overage above a plan's included amount). Most teams run more than one tool, so list every tool first. Pick yours below. The rest of the guide uses these answers.

Why AI spend turns into a black box

Each tool bills separately, often on a company card or a single admin account. Finance sees a handful of invoices. Nobody sees which developer, team or project the spend belongs to, and shared logins hide it further.

03

Turn tokens into dollars

Usage-billed tools charge by tokens, the units of text sent to and returned by the model. A long agent session that reads a codebase can use hundreds of thousands of tokens. The formula for one developer's monthly usage cost is:

sessions per day × tokens per session × working days × price per token

    Questions about AI coding cost tracking

    Are tracked cost figures exact?

    Token counts can be exact when they come from session history. Dollar amounts are estimates built from list or contract prices, so treat them as a guide for decisions. Your vendor's invoice stays the source of truth for what you paid.

    What is the difference between seat cost and usage cost?

    Seat cost is a flat monthly price per developer. Usage cost depends on tokens consumed, either on pay-per-token plans or as overage above an included amount.

    How do I track AI cost per project?

    Tie each AI session to the repository it ran in, then add up tokens per repository and map repositories to projects. Manual tagging works for small teams. Automatic session capture scales better.

    How often should I review AI coding spend?

    Monthly for budgets and billing, weekly while you are rolling out a new tool or plan, so spikes and unused seats show up early.
